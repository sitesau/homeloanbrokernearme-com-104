Week one: name the lending job

Start with purpose, not postcode. The listed categories are home loan brokers, first home buyer brokers, refinance brokers, investment loan brokers, and construction and specialist lending. Each category changes the evidence worth preparing. A first purchase leans on deposit, income and purchase detail. A refinance needs the current lender, repayment type and reason for review. An investment discussion should separate personal borrowing capacity from rental assumptions. Construction borrowing may involve contract terms, progress payments and different lender treatment.

  • Buying: purchase range, deposit source, income evidence and costs outside the loan.
  • Refinancing: remaining balance, current features, exit or switching costs and objective.
  • Investing: existing debts, rent assumptions and whether negative gearing is even relevant.
  • Construction: building contract, milestones, valuation expectations and contingency.

Naming the job early keeps locality useful but secondary.

Week two: assemble the fact pattern

The second step is a simple evidence file. List income sources, expenses, current debts, deposit or equity, property status, approximate amount and timing pressure. If refinancing, add the lender, repayment type and why the loan is being reviewed. If investing, note expected rent separately from personal capacity. If timing is urgent because of auction bidding, contract exchange or a fixed rate expiry, record that too.

The source material describes submission as free and without obligation, but it also says details may be shared with suitable professionals. That makes specificity protective. A borrower who knows the file can ask sharper questions and is less likely to treat the form as the decision itself.

ASIC Moneysmart encourages borrowers to understand home loan features, costs and risks before choosing a loan. That guidance fits naturally here: the timeline is for understanding, not for skipping professional assessment.

Week three: screen listings without over-trusting them

Listings can screen options, but they cannot prove fit. Use category, location served, services named on the profile, reviews, photos and pricing as early signals only. Repeated profile language is a prompt to verify current facts directly, not evidence of current lender access, eligibility, fees or availability.

A disciplined screen asks whether the broker regularly handles the relevant loan type and whether the next conversation will cover documents, fees, lender panel, conflicts and unsuitable options. This is where a second resource helps: what to ask before submitting an enquiry can sit beside the listing details as a checklist.

Locality still matters, yet it should narrow service area, travel and property familiarity rather than substitute for evidence. A suburb name cannot confirm experience with the exact loan purpose.

Decision week: compare two conversations

When two brokers appear relevant, compare how each handles evidence. A stronger conversation asks about income, expenses, debts, deposit, property purpose and future plans before lender choice. A weaker one moves quickly to a single loan type without explaining assumptions.

  1. Check whether the category matches the job already named.
  2. Confirm the documents needed before recommendations are framed.
  3. Ask which lenders are considered and how conflicts are disclosed.
  4. Clarify fees, timing and how unsuitable options would be explained.
  5. Verify lender access, eligibility and availability directly.
